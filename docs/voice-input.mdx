The chat composer has a microphone button beside Send. Tap it to start recording, tap it again to stop: the recording is transcribed locally and the text is inserted into the composer, where it stays editable before sending. Typed input keeps working exactly as before, whether or not transcription is available.

Transcription runs fully on your own hardware. The browser encodes the recording as 16 kHz mono WAV and posts it to the agent server (`POST /api/v1/voice/transcribe`); the server forwards the bytes to the `aman-stt` sidecar, which runs Whisper `small` (int8, CPU) via faster-whisper and returns the transcript. The browser never talks to the sidecar directly, and no audio or transcript ever leaves your machine. When the sidecar is unreachable, the mic button shows as unavailable and everything else keeps working.

## Ways to talk

- **Tap to record.** Tap the mic, talk, and tap it again. Your words go into the message box so you can check them before you send. If nothing was heard, the mic says so instead of leaving the box empty with no word. A recording also ends if you switch chats or the app moves to the background, and the mic says so then too, so a recording never disappears without a word.
- **Hold to talk.** Hold the mic, talk, and let go. Your words are sent as soon as they are written down.
- **Hold-to-talk keys.** On a computer, hold Ctrl + Shift + Space while you talk, and let go to send. You can pick other keys, or turn them off, in Settings, Themes, under "Talking to Aman". The choice is saved in this browser only.
- **Voice mode.** Tap the voice mode button beside the mic, then just talk. The voice mode button shows sound waves, and the mic shows a microphone. Point at either button, or move to it with the Tab key, to see what it does. Aman writes down what you say when you pause (if you speak again before two earlier phrases are written down, it shows that it missed that, and says so too when it is not already talking, and you say it again), answers by voice, and keeps listening. Talk while it is answering and it stops to listen. Say "yes, send it", "yes, approve" or "yes, delete it" to answer an approval card after Aman reads it back; see "Approving by voice" below. The button shows Listening, Thinking or Speaking, and the avatar follows. Tap the button again to end it. It also ends when you switch chats, leave the page, hide the tab, take the mic by hand, or when the connection closes. It ends by itself after 2 minutes in which you say nothing (a long answer or a long task is not counted as quiet), after 30 minutes however much you talk, and when the sound arrives faster than a microphone can make it, which means it could not keep up; Aman tells you why, and you tap the button to start again. It is never on unless you turn it on, and it only listens in the chat where you turned it on.
- **Paste.** Choose "Paste from clipboard" in the `+` menu to add copied text to your message. To copy an answer, use the copy button under it.

## Where your voice goes

This section is the voice feature's data-flow record.

- **Your voice.** The browser records only while the mic is on: while you record, hold, or have voice mode on. The recording goes to the Aman server on your computer, which passes it to `aman-stt` and gets the words back. The recording is not saved.
- **Voice mode.** The browser sends your microphone sound to the Aman server over one private connection, which only works for the person who opened it, only from Aman's own page, and lasts at most 30 seconds before it is used. The server decides from the sound whether you are talking, so a forgotten tab cannot keep the microphone open past the 2 minute and 30 minute limits. The server finds where each sentence starts and ends with a small speech model running on your computer, passes each sentence to `aman-stt`, and sends the answer's audio back as it is made. Nothing is saved: not the sound, and not the words except as the normal message in your chat. The microphone is asked to cancel echo and noise, so it does not hear the answer coming out of your speakers.
- **The answer read aloud.** The answer's text goes to the Aman server, which passes it to `aman-tts` and sends the audio back. When `aman-tts` is not running, your browser's built-in voice reads it instead, and the text stays in the browser.
- **What never leaves your computer.** Your recordings, the words written down from them, and the audio of the answers. No speech service on the internet is called.
- **What is kept.** Only the messages themselves, like any typed message. The server logs sizes, timings and status codes, never what was said.

## Approving by voice

When Aman needs your approval to do something, such as send an email, it reads out everything the card will do, including the subject line of an email, and waits. Say exactly "yes, send it" (for an email), "yes, approve", or "yes, delete it" (for deleting a scheduled task), or say "no". Say the whole phrase in one go. A short stop between the words is fine ("Yes. Send it."), but a different wording, a sentence with more words around the phrase, a phrase that ends as a question or trails off, or silence, does not approve. Words you said before Aman finished reading the card out are never an answer to it. If you say something else while a card is waiting, Aman reminds you once, and after that treats your words as a new message and leaves the card in the app. Aman never takes an answer while it is talking, or in the second and a half after it stops, and it ignores sound that matches what it just said. If your answer comes too soon, it asks you to say it again.

The answer is the same one the app's buttons give, for that one time only. If the card changed or timed out, voice refuses and tells you. After it acts, it says what it did.

Voice reads only the kinds of card it can read in full and be sure you heard. Everything else is left on the screen. That means: an email with no hidden copy and no new recipient, a new calendar event with no guests (its time zone and calendar are read too), and deleting a scheduled task (read as deleted for good). A card is also left on the screen when it is long, or when the voice would say any part of it differently from how it is written, such as a web link, a code block, a list, a number sign or range, a folder path, a number like "$1,5" or "10.0.0.1", or any word that is not plain English letters (for example "café" or text in another language). The voice itself decides this, in the same step that speaks the card: the whole card goes to the voice as one text, and nothing is cut out first, so a hidden part cannot slip past. If the voice would not say any part of it as written, none of it is read and Aman says "I can't read that one out exactly, so I've left it in the app for you to approve." If the voice cannot be reached, or fails part way, the card stays on the screen too, and a part that was already spoken stays heard. If the voice service is out of date, Aman shows "The voice service is out of date. Run aman upgrade, then try again." on the screen, and says only that it left the card in the app. Two numbers written side by side with only a bracket, quote, row of dots or other silent sign between them (such as "1000 (500)", "19, 99", "twenty (5)", "1000 and 500" or "1000 & 500"), or a list like "Items 1, 2 and 3", also stay on the screen. So do two numbers that could run into one, such as "1000 'five hundred'". A card with "1000 and 500" stays because that is heard as one amount, "one thousand and five hundred". A date or time written with a comma after a month or weekday ("Fri Nov 6, 2:30" or "March 5, 2026"), a range ("Between 100 and 150", heard as two numbers), "5 million" and "the 14th" are read. A web address written inside a message ("my.bank.com") stays on the screen; letters with dots, such as "p.m.", "e.g." or "Ph.D.", are read letter by letter. A name, title or subject that holds a line break also stays on the screen. Every real address and field is heard, in a fixed order: Aman first says how many addresses, events or schedules the card holds, then each address, then each name, title or subject last on a line of its own after a label such as "The subject is" or "The title is", and an address is said before the name next to it ("To eve at evil dot com, named Victor"). A name or title is read as written, so one that sounds like another line is still read. It can add words, but it cannot hide or replace anything real: an extra address would not match the count you heard first, and an event or schedule card ends with a line that says the real time again after all the names and titles ("Once more, the event is on Fri Nov 6, 2:30 to 3:00 pm"). The name an email is sent as, a note that a name was left off an address, a duplicate event and an event that falls on several days are read out too, and dots and hyphens in an address are said as "dot" and "dash". When an email is a reply, the message it answers is shown on the screen, not read. A time zone written as an offset, such as Etc/GMT+5, stays on the screen. Shell commands, spending money, file changes (including saving a file outside your workspace), plans, new scheduled tasks, exact connector settings, a new helper persona, and any card that is not one of the kinds above are always answered on the screen. So is any card that holds the answer phrase itself, and any case where more than one card is waiting. For these, Aman says it left the card in the app.

If Aman could not read all of a card out loud, for example because the voice failed part way, or you cut it off by talking, the card is left in the app and a "yes" does nothing. A card you answer on the screen while Aman is waiting is not waiting any more: the next thing you say is a new message.

Each person has two voice requests in flight at a time, shared between voice mode and replies read aloud by hand. If you read a reply aloud while Aman is talking in voice mode, one waits for the other. Voice mode can be started 10 times a minute. Each person has one conversation open per account they sign in to, and at most 3 in all (a new one on the same account replaces the old).

## Voice mode settings

- `TTS_ENABLED` (default `true`). Set `false` to turn the voice on your computer off. Voice mode will not start, and replies you read aloud by hand use your browser's voice. Voice mode also will not start while the voice container is not answering; the message says to try again in a minute.
- `VOICE_VAD_MODEL_DIR` (default `<app data folder>/voice-model`; the images set `/opt/aman-voice`). Where the speech-detection model is. The Aman image carries this 2 MB file, checked against a fixed fingerprint when the image is built, so voice mode makes no download and needs nothing set up. If the file is missing or does not match, voice mode refuses to start. For host development, `pnpm dev` fetches it by itself when you are online. If you were offline, run `pnpm voice:model` once you are back online. `pnpm test` keeps its own copy in `packages/trueforge/.cache/voice-model` and fetches it first.

Voice mode keeps its tickets in the memory of one server, so run a single server copy. A person can start voice mode 10 times a minute, and the server holds at most 20 voice conversations at once.

## Deployment

`docker-compose.yml` runs `aman-stt` as a sidecar next to the server:

- Built from `ops/aman-stt` (Python 3.11 slim, CPU-only; the image has no CUDA userspace and the app pins `device=cpu`).
- Pinned to cores `16-19` with a 2 GB memory limit, so transcription work never contends with chat or embedding work, which stay on cores `0-15`.
- No host port is published; the sidecar is reachable only inside the compose network at `http://aman-stt:8001`.
- The Whisper model (~464 MB) downloads once at first startup into the persistent `stt-model-cache` volume; container recreates reuse it.
- `restart: unless-stopped`, matching the server.

The server reads the sidecar address from `STT_URL` (default `http://aman-stt:8001`; compose sets it explicitly). The server does not wait for the sidecar to be healthy before starting: typed chat stays available when transcription is down.

## Host development

`pnpm dev:infra` starts `aman-stt` from `docker-compose.dev.yml` next to Postgres and Redis, while the server and UI run on the host with `pnpm dev`. The service uses the same build, image, memory limit and thread count as the full stack, with two differences: it publishes `127.0.0.1:8001` (loopback only, since the sidecar has no authentication) and it has no `cpuset` pin, because the host-run server cannot be pinned to a disjoint core set and a fixed range would fail on hosts with fewer than 20 cores. `pnpm dev:infra` rebuilds the images each time it starts, so a voice service from an older build is never reused. The first run builds the image and downloads the model (~464 MB) into the dev project's own `stt-model-cache` volume, separate from the full stack's.

Point the server at the published port by setting `STT_URL=http://localhost:8001` in `packages/trueforge/.env` (already present in `packages/trueforge/.env.example`), then restart `pnpm dev`. Without it the server looks for `http://aman-stt:8001`, which only resolves inside the compose network, and the mic button stays hidden.

## Tuning

**Fewer or more CPU cores.** The `16-19` / `0-15` split assumes a 20-core host. On a host with a different core count, move both pins together and keep them disjoint: give the sidecar 4 cores and the server the rest. For example, on a 12-core host, pin `aman-stt` to `8-11` and the server to `0-7`. The server pin lives on the `server` service (`cpuset`), the sidecar pin on `aman-stt`.

**Transcription threads.** `AMAN_STT_CPU_THREADS` (default `4`) sets the Whisper encoder thread count; keep it equal to the number of cores pinned to the sidecar.

**Model size.** `AMAN_STT_MODEL` (default `small`) selects the Whisper model; `AMAN_STT_FALLBACK_MODEL` (default `base`) is used when the primary fails to load. Larger models transcribe more accurately and need more RAM; raise the service `mem_limit` accordingly.

**Memory.** The default 2 GB limit covers the `small` int8 model with headroom. If the container is OOM-killed during warmup, raise `mem_limit` before changing anything else.

**Request limits.** The sidecar caps audio bodies at 30 MB (`AMAN_STT_MAX_BODY_BYTES`); the server caps them at 30 MB, which is about 16 minutes of 16 kHz mono 16-bit audio. The browser holds recordings to 3 minutes. Transcription requests time out after 120 seconds server-side.

## Spoken replies: the voice on your computer

Replies can be read aloud. When the voice container is running and `TTS_ENABLED` is not `false`, replies are read by a voice that runs on your own computer. When it is not, your browser's built-in voice reads them instead.

The reply to a message you spoke is read aloud by itself when it is done, unless you turned off "Speak replies when I use my voice". When it will be read aloud, Aman is told, so it answers in a few short, plain sentences without lists or tables. To hear any other reply, tap the speaker button under it.

**Choosing the voice.** Pick one of six voices in Settings, Themes, under "Voice replies". Heart is the default. Picking a voice plays a short sample. The choice is saved in this browser only.

**What it is.** A small container called `aman-tts`. It runs the Kokoro voice model (Apache-2.0) on the computer's processor and turns reply text into audio. The model and its voices are built into the container, so it never goes online.

**Nothing leaves your computer.** The browser sends the reply text to the server, which passes it to `aman-tts` and sends the audio back. The text and the audio stay on your computer, and neither is saved or written to the logs.

**Limits.** One request reads at most 4,000 characters, and each person can ask for 30 readings a minute. Longer replies are read in parts.

**How to turn it off.** Stop the container, and replies are read by your browser's voice instead:

- Installed with the installer: run `docker stop aman-tts`. It starts again the next time the installer or `aman upgrade` runs.
- With `docker-compose.yml`: run `docker compose stop aman-tts`.
- With `pnpm dev:infra`: remove the `aman-tts` service from `docker-compose.dev.yml`, or stop it with `docker compose -f docker-compose.dev.yml stop aman-tts`.

For host development, set `TTS_URL=http://localhost:8002` in `packages/trueforge/.env` (already in `packages/trueforge/.env.example`). The server reads the address from `TTS_URL`, which defaults to `http://aman-tts:8002`.
